# Sources — journey3d "What happens to your coffee after you drink it?" (2026-11-22)
- StatPearls, Caffeine — https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/18756 — "Caffeine is primarily metabolized in the liver via the cytochrome P450 oxidase system, specifically the enzyme CYP1A2";
  "caffeine easily crosses the blood-brain barrier and antagonizes all 4 adenosine receptor subtypes"; "the half-life of caffeine in the average adult is approximately 5 hours";
  "caffeine is a potent stimulator of gastric acid secretion and gastrointestinal motility"; metabolites "are then further metabolized and excreted in the urine".
- CDC, 2nd/Biochemical Indicators report — Caffeine — https://cdc.gov/nutrition-report/media/pdfs/2026/06/Bioactive-Caffeine-508.pdf — "Gastrointestinal absorption is typically complete within 45 minutes";
  "Only a small fraction (2%) of caffeine is excreted unmetabolized"; CYP1A2 in the liver.
- EFSA explains caffeine — https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/corporate_publications/files/efsaexplainscaffeine150527.pdf — "average half-life is approximately four hours, with a range of two to eight hours".
- Harvard T.H. Chan Nutrition Source, Caffeine — https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/caffeine/ — "absorbed within about 45 minutes ... peaks in the blood anywhere from 15 minutes to 2 hours".
- Frontiers in Pharmacology 2022 review (caffeine metabolism) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2021.752826/pdf — "negligible first-pass metabolism";
  paraxanthine "around 80–90% of caffeine demethylation"; "3% or less being excreted unchanged in urine".
- IJMS 2024 review — https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/25/22/12003 — ~20% absorbed in the stomach, the rest in the small intestine; ~45 min.
- OpenStax A&P 2e 23.6 — https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder — "all blood from the alimentary canal passes through the liver"; hepatic portal vein.
- MedlinePlus, Caffeine — https://medlineplus.gov/caffeine.html — caffeine "Increases the release of acid in your stomach".
