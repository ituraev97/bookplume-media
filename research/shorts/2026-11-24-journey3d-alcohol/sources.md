# Sources — journey3d "Where does alcohol go after you drink it?" (2026-11-24)
- BMJ 2005;330:85-87, Paton, "Alcohol in the body" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC543875 — "relatively slowly absorbed from the stomach, more rapidly absorbed from the small intestine"; "water soluble"; "distributed throughout the water in the body"; "On an empty stomach blood alcohol concentration peaks about one hour after consumption"; "blood is received direct from the stomach and small bowel via the portal vein"; "oxidation by alcohol dehydrogenases" → acetaldehyde → "aldehyde dehydrogenases to ... acetate"; "More than 90% of alcohol is eliminated by the liver"; "2-5% is excreted unchanged in urine, sweat, or breath."
- Duke APEP, How is alcohol eliminated from the body — https://sites.duke.edu/apep/module-1-gender-matters/science-content/how-is-alcohol-eliminated-from-the-body — ADH in liver cells, ALDH → acetic acid, "primary site of metabolism is in the liver", alcohol "reaches all tissues in the body, including the brain".
- Physiological Reviews 2025, "Recent advances in alcohol metabolism: from the gut to the brain" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12345593/ — "absorbed ... slowly from the stomach and more rapidly from the small intestine ... due to the large surface area created by the presence of villi"; 94–98% oxidised in the liver; 2–6% excreted unchanged (breath, urine, sweat).
