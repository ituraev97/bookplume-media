What happens in your body when you swallow? Follow one bite second by second, from your teeth to your stomach: the oral, pharyngeal and esophageal phases of swallowing (deglutition), and the muscles and cranial nerves behind each step.
Air and food share one tube, the pharynx. In about one second your body closes your nose, pauses breathing, shuts your vocal folds, lifts your larynx and tips the epiglottis down. Here is how.

What you'll remember:
• 3 phases of swallowing: oral (the only voluntary one), pharyngeal (a reflex, about 1 second), esophageal (peristalsis, about 4–8 s for solids)
• Airway protection: soft palate + uvula rise, swallowing apnea, vocal folds close, hyoid + larynx move up and forward, epiglottis tips down
• The gate: upper esophageal sphincter (cricopharyngeus) and the lower esophageal sphincter
• 5 cranial nerves: V trigeminal, VII facial, IX glossopharyngeal, X vagus, XII hypoglossal
• Exam odd ones out: palatoglossus = the only tongue muscle on the vagus; stylopharyngeus = the only pharyngeal muscle on the glossopharyngeal
