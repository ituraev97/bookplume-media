What happens in your body when you hold your breath? Take a breath, hold it, and watch the timer: second by second, from your diaphragm and lungs to the chemoreceptors in your brainstem that finally force you to breathe.
Respiratory physiology for anatomy & physiology, nursing and med school exams: phrenic nerve C3–C5, alveoli and gas exchange, central vs peripheral chemoreceptors, the medullary respiratory centers and the breaking point. Quick quiz at the end. Comment your breath-hold time and your score!

What you'll learn:
• How the diaphragm and external intercostals pull air in, and why the phrenic nerve (C3, 4, 5) matters
• Why gas exchange in the alveoli keeps going while you hold your breath
• Why the urge to breathe comes from rising CO2 (pH), not low oxygen — central chemoreceptors in the medulla vs carotid bodies (IX) and aortic bodies (X)
• Who runs breathing: the dorsal and ventral respiratory groups, the pons, and your cortex overriding them
• The breaking point and "struggle phase", and why hyperventilating before swimming underwater is dangerous (CDC)
