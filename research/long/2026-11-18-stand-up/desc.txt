What happens in your body when you stand up too fast? Second by second: gravity pulls 300–800 mL of blood into your legs, baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch sound the alarm, and your brainstem fixes your blood pressure in about 15 seconds.
Orthostatic physiology for anatomy & physiology, nursing and med school exams: baroreceptor reflex, cranial nerves IX and X, the medulla, sympathetic vs vagal output, MAP = CO × SVR. Quick quiz at the end. Comment your score!

What you'll learn:
• Why blood pools in your legs and gut veins when you stand (veins hold about 64% of your blood)
• Where the arterial baroreceptors are (carotid sinus + aortic arch) and which nerves carry their signals (glossopharyngeal IX, vagus X)
• How the nucleus of the solitary tract and the medullary cardiovascular centers raise heart rate, contractility and vascular resistance
• Why the head rush peaks around 10 seconds, and how the muscle pump and respiratory pump help
• The textbook definition of orthostatic hypotension (≥ 20 systolic or ≥ 10 diastolic mmHg within 3 minutes)
