Eye anatomy made easy: learn the 3 layers of the eyeball (sclera + cornea, choroid + ciliary body + iris, retina), the lens, the chambers, aqueous and vitreous humor, and the 6 extraocular muscles with their nerves — in about 9 minutes.
Why is the optic disc a blind spot? What makes the aqueous humor? Learn it with clean memory tricks (S-C-R, Cones for Color, LR6 SO4 R3), then test yourself with a 5-question quiz for nursing school, med school and A&P exams.

Key takeaways:
• 3 layers, outside → in: fibrous (sclera = the white, about 5/6 of the eyeball; cornea = the clear front 1/6 that bends light), vascular / uvea (choroid, ciliary body, iris), neural (retina).
• Retina: rods = dim light, cones = bright light + color. Signal: photoreceptors → bipolar cells → ganglion cells. Macula with the fovea = sharpest vision; optic disc = no photoreceptors = blind spot.
• Lens: held by zonular fibers (suspensory ligaments) to the ciliary body; the ciliary muscle changes its shape to focus. A cloudy lens = cataract.
• Aqueous humor: made by the ciliary processes in the posterior chamber → through the pupil → anterior chamber → trabecular meshwork → canal of Schlemm. Vitreous humor = clear gel behind the lens.
• 6 extraocular muscles: 4 recti from the common tendinous ring + superior oblique (through the trochlea) + inferior oblique. LR6 SO4 R3: lateral rectus = abducens (CN VI), superior oblique = trochlear (CN IV), the rest = oculomotor (CN III).
