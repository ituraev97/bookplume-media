Key takeaways:
• 3 bones meet at the knee: femur, tibia and patella (the largest sesamoid bone). The fibula is NOT part of the knee joint.
• The knee is 3 joints in 1 capsule: medial tibiofemoral, lateral tibiofemoral and patellofemoral — a synovial, mainly hinge joint.
• Menisci = C-shaped fibrocartilage pads that deepen the tibia and absorb shock. The medial meniscus is attached to the MCL and capsule (less mobile, injured more often); the lateral is nearly circular and more mobile.
• Collaterals stop sideways bending: MCL resists valgus stress, LCL (to the fibular head) resists varus stress. Cruciates are named for their tibial attachment: the ACL stops the tibia sliding forward, the PCL (the stronger one) stops it sliding backward.
• LAMP: Lateral condyle → Anterior cruciate, Medial condyle → Posterior cruciate. The popliteus unlocks the fully straightened knee.
