Key takeaways:
• 3 bones: clavicle (the only long bone that lies horizontally), scapula (spine, acromion, coracoid process, glenoid cavity), humerus (head, greater + lesser tubercles, intertubercular groove).
• 4 joints: sternoclavicular (the only bony link between the arm and the axial skeleton), acromioclavicular (held mainly by the coracoclavicular ligament), scapulothoracic (not a true joint), glenohumeral (the "shoulder joint").
• The glenohumeral joint is a shallow ball and socket: the glenoid covers less than 1/3 of the humeral head; the glenoid labrum deepens it, the coracoacromial arch sits above it, the subacromial bursa lets the supraspinatus tendon glide.
• SITS: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor → greater tubercle; subscapularis → lesser tubercle. Supraspinatus starts abduction (0–15°), infraspinatus + teres minor rotate laterally, subscapularis rotates medially, and all four hold the humeral head in the socket.
• Nerves: suprascapular (supraspinatus, infraspinatus), axillary (teres minor + deltoid), subscapular nerves (subscapularis). Abduction: supraspinatus 0–15°, middle deltoid 15–90°, trapezius + serratus anterior rotate the scapula above 90°.
