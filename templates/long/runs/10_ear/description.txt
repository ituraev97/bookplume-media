Ear anatomy made easy: learn the outer, middle and inner ear — auricle, ear canal, eardrum, the 3 ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes), the auditory tube, the cochlea, and the balance organs — in about 9 minutes.

Key takeaways:
• 3 parts: the OUTER ear collects sound and ends at the eardrum; the MIDDLE ear is an air-filled space with 3 ossicles; the INNER ear is fluid-filled and holds the organs of hearing and balance. Middle + inner ear sit in the temporal bone.
• Outer ear: auricle (pinna) = elastic cartilage + skin, except the lobule. Ear canal (external acoustic meatus) ≈ 2.5 cm, S-shaped, outer ⅓ cartilage, inner ⅔ bone; cerumen = earwax. Eardrum: umbo + cone of light.
• Middle ear: malleus → incus → stapes (Many Ice Skaters); the stapes footplate sits in the oval window. Tensor tympani = mandibular branch of the trigeminal; stapedius = facial nerve (CN VII). The auditory (Eustachian) tube connects to the nasopharynx and equalizes pressure.
• Inner ear: bony labyrinth (cochlea, vestibule, 3 semicircular canals) with perilymph; membranous labyrinth (cochlear duct, utricle, saccule, semicircular ducts) with endolymph.
• Hearing: oval window → scala vestibuli → apex → scala tympani → round window; organ of Corti on the basilar membrane; high pitch at the base, low pitch at the apex. Balance: utricle + saccule = head position + linear acceleration; semicircular canals = rotation. All via CN VIII (vestibulocochlear nerve).
