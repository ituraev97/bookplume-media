Muscles of the hand made easy: learn all 19 intrinsic hand muscles in about 10 minutes, thenar muscles, hypothenar muscles, adductor pollicis, lumbricals and the palmar and dorsal interossei.
Which nerve runs your thumb muscles? It's a trick question: learn the median vs ulnar nerve supply with LOAF, PAD & DAB, then test yourself with a 5-question quiz for A&P, nursing school and med school exams.

Key takeaways:
• Intrinsic hand muscles start and end inside the hand (precision); extrinsic muscles sit in the forearm and send long tendons (power).
• Thenar group: abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis, all from the flexor retinaculum, scaphoid and trapezium; recurrent branch of the median nerve. Adductor pollicis (oblique + transverse heads) is ulnar.
• Hypothenar group mirrors it: abductor, flexor and opponens digiti minimi (pisiform, hook of hamate), deep branch of the ulnar nerve; the ulnar nerve enters through Guyon's canal.
• 4 lumbricals arise from the flexor digitorum profundus tendons, flex the MCP joints and extend the IP joints; lumbricals 1–2 median, 3–4 ulnar.
• Interossei: PAD (3 palmar adduct) and DAB (4 dorsal abduct) around the middle finger, all deep branch of the ulnar nerve. Median nerve = LOAF; everything else ulnar; radial supplies none.
