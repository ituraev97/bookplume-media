# Sources — femoral artery 6 branches mnemonic short (2026-11-27, 19:31 run)
- Kenhub, Femoral artery: https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/femoral-artery — "five branches in the femoral triangle and one in the adductor canal, six in total"; mnemonic "Do Princesses Sew Sweet Superhero Dresses?" (D descending genicular, P profunda femoris, S superficial epigastric, S superficial circumflex iliac, S superficial external pudendal, D deep external pudendal); profunda = largest branch, ~3.5 cm below the inguinal ligament; superficial circumflex iliac = smallest; superficial epigastric ~1 cm below the ligament; descending genicular in the adductor canal (vastus medialis, adductor magnus, proximomedial thigh skin); external pudendals (lower abdominal + penile/scrotal/labial skin; perineal + scrotal/labium majus skin).
- Wikipedia, Femoral artery / Deep artery of the thigh / Descending genicular artery (also known as highest genicular): profunda gives the medial + lateral circumflex femoral arteries; superficial epigastric crosses the inguinal ligament toward the umbilicus.
- Gray's Anatomy 1918 Fig. 548 "Scheme of the femoral artery" (archive.org anatomyofhumanbo00grayrich), printed labels incl. old 'pudic' and 'femoral profunda'. 'Anastomotica magna' on the plate NOT claimed as a synonym (unverified).
